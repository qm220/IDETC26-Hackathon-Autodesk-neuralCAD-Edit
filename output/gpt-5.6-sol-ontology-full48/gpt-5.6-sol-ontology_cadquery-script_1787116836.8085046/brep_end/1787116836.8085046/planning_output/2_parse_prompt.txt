An engineer has requested an edit to the model with the following natural language request:
```
Add a 2 millimetre fillet to the edges of the scrolling wheel slot to ensure a smooth surface.
```

First categorize each word in the request into four categories:
1. Action words, describing a geometric operation, a new feature to create, or a numerical parameter of that operation.
2. Referenced features, referring to existing geometry, features, entities, or relationships that must be resolved from the original CAD model.
3. Intention, describing the engineering purpose, desired function, or desired behavior of the modification.
4. grammatical words, articles, conjunctions, prepositions, and other words without independent engineering meaning. Do not discard relational words that may have engineering meaning such as "connecting", "between", "through", "across", "adjacent", or "aligned". Preserve them as geometric/relational constraints when they affect how the edit should be performed.

Then, decompose the request into separate CAD actions.

For each action, extract:

operation: the geometric modification to perform.
Geometric parameters explicitly stated in the request, such as diameter, radius, depth, thickness, size, angle, or distance.
target: geometry explicitly referenced by the request, if any. It can be geometries from the original geometry or geometries created from previous actions.
relational_constraint: spatial or topological relationships that constrain the operation.
unresolved_reference: what must be identified from the CAD model when the wording implies a relationship but does not explicitly identify the participating geometry.

Do NOT invent geometry that is not specified in the request. If the request contains multiple modifications joined by words such as "and", represent them as separate actions.

Additionally, extract the engineering purpose separately from the geometric operations. The intention should explain why the modification is requested, not introduce additional geometry.
